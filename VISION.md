# Vision

Lev Review helps local businesses turn customer follow-up into a simple reputation workflow. A business shares one link. Happy customers continue to Google. Unhappy customers get a private path where the operator can understand and recover the experience before it becomes a public review.

## Direction

The product should become a quiet operating system for reputation intake across one or many locations. It should stay easy enough for a restaurant owner, clinic manager, contractor, or front-desk operator to set up without a sales call, while becoming useful enough that review recovery becomes part of the daily routine.

The long-term product is not just a review link generator. It is a location-aware workflow for asking, routing, learning, and following up.

## Current Focus

- Make the core routing loop reliable: location setup, customer rating, Google redirect, private feedback capture, and dashboard review.
- Make the first-run experience clear: users should know what to enter, what link to share, and what will happen for each rating.
- Keep trust high through plain pricing, clean SEO pages, email verification, secure defaults, and no manipulative review behavior.
- Preserve the distinctive visual direction: calm, precise, modern, and operator-focused.

## Product Principles

- One link, two outcomes.
- Public review requests should be fast for satisfied customers.
- Private feedback should feel respectful and useful, not like a dark pattern.
- Each location should remain a first-class object because local businesses often operate by storefront, branch, service area, or office.
- Operators need less noise, not more dashboards. Show the next useful action and the facts needed to take it.
- Paid features should map to operational value: richer recovery details, customization, prioritization, and multi-location usefulness.

## What We Should Build Toward

- A stronger dashboard for reviewing private feedback and acting on it.
- Better share surfaces: QR codes, printable assets, email snippets, and link copying.
- Clearer location-level settings, especially rating thresholds and Google Place data.
- Better onboarding for non-technical users who do not know what a Google Place ID is.
- Safer billing and subscription flows that make plan state obvious.
- A progressively more consistent design system across marketing, dashboard, auth, and review capture pages.

## What We Should Not Build

- Tools for faking, buying, hiding, or manipulating Google reviews.
- A generic CRM, helpdesk, or survey platform unless it directly strengthens review recovery.
- Heavy AI response automation that sends customer-facing messages without operator control.
- Broad integrations that add maintenance burden before the core workflow is excellent.
- Decorative dashboards that look impressive but do not help a local operator take action.

## Quality Bar

Every meaningful change should make the product easier to trust, easier to understand, or easier to operate. If a feature adds setup burden, privacy risk, billing confusion, or visual noise, it needs a strong product reason.
